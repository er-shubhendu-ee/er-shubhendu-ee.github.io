Overview

Grid-Connected Inverter Research

Master's research focused on the modeling, control, synchronization, and stability analysis of single-phase grid-connected voltage source inverters. The work investigated practical challenges associated with renewable energy integration, grid synchronization, harmonic mitigation, and dynamic response under varying operating conditions.

The project combined mathematical modeling, simulation, control-system design, and performance evaluation to study inverter behavior under realistic grid conditions.

Research Areas

Scope of Work

  • Single-phase grid-connected inverter systems.
  • Synchronization using Phase-Locked Loop (PLL) techniques.
  • Current control and voltage control strategies.
  • LCL filter modeling and design.
  • Grid disturbance analysis.
  • Harmonic mitigation techniques.
  • Dynamic stability evaluation.
  • Renewable energy integration studies.

Modeling

System Development

Mathematical models were developed for inverter switching behavior, output filtering stages, synchronization loops, and grid interaction. Small-signal and dynamic analyses were performed to understand system stability and transient response.

Special emphasis was placed on the interaction between inverter control loops and the utility grid under both normal and disturbed operating conditions.

Control Systems

Control Strategy Investigation

  • Phase-Locked Loop (PLL) design and analysis.
  • Reference frame synchronization techniques.
  • Current regulation methods.
  • Voltage control mechanisms.
  • Digital controller implementation concepts.
  • Transient and steady-state performance evaluation.

Tools

Software & Analysis Platforms

  • MATLAB
  • Simulink
  • PLECS
  • PSIM
  • LaTeX
  • Control System Toolbox
  • Power Electronics Simulation Tools

Outcome

Research Contributions

The research provided insight into synchronization performance, inverter-grid interaction, controller design considerations, and stability characteristics of grid-connected converter systems. The resulting work contributes to the broader understanding of renewable energy interfacing and modern power electronic conversion systems.

Engineering Perspective

Hardware & Firmware Considerations

While the primary focus of this research was system modeling and control, the work was continuously evaluated from a practical implementation perspective. Particular attention was given to the challenges involved in translating simulation results into real-world embedded inverter systems.

  • Microcontroller-based digital control implementation.
  • PWM generation and switching strategy considerations.
  • Voltage and current sensing architectures.
  • ADC sampling synchronization with control loops.
  • Real-time execution constraints and timing analysis.
  • Isolation, gate driving, and power-stage interfacing.
  • Protection mechanisms including over-current and fault handling.
  • Embedded implementation of PLL and control algorithms.

This practical perspective was influenced by professional experience in embedded systems and firmware development, allowing the research to consider not only theoretical performance but also implementation feasibility, computational requirements, and hardware limitations.
